Thirteen

Privacy policy

Effective 20 October 2026. Applies to Thirteen on the web at thirteen.haugas.dev, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and Windows.

Thirteen collects no data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your events and settings stay on your devices and in the calendar accounts you already use, and Thirteen never sends them to us or to anyone else.

What Thirteen collects

Nothing. Thirteen does not collect, store on any server, sell or share any personal information. It has no sign-in, no analytics or advertising code, and no third-party libraries that send data anywhere.

Your calendars and events

iPhone, iPad and Mac: if you allow it, Thirteen reads and changes the events in the calendars on your device through Apple's calendar system. Those calendars sync with iCloud, Google, Exchange or other accounts exactly as they do for Apple's Calendar app, under those services' own terms. Thirteen itself sends nothing. Calendars you subscribe to in your device's settings are downloaded by the system, not by Thirteen. You can turn access off at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Calendars (on a Mac, in System Settings). Without access, Thirteen shows no events and everything else still works.

Android: if you allow it, Thirteen reads and changes the events in your phone's calendars through Android's calendar storage. Your accounts' own sync (for example Google) moves them, not Thirteen. If no calendar on your phone can be written and you ask for one, Thirteen creates a calendar named Thirteen on the phone; its events stay on the phone and are not synced or backed up. You can turn access off in your phone's Settings under Apps > Thirteen > Permissions.

Windows and the website: Thirteen keeps its own calendars there. The events you create are saved only on your computer (Windows) or in your browser's storage for this site (web), and they are not synced to your other devices. Calendar files you import are read on your device, and the files you export are saved where you choose. Nothing is uploaded. Browsers may delete a site's storage on their own: Safari does so when you haven't opened a site for about a week, unless you added it to your Dock or Home Screen. Export your calendars to keep a copy.

Network connections

Apps for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android: they make no network connections. Calendar syncing is done by your device's own accounts. The Android app does not even have the permission to use the internet, and the Mac app's sandbox does not allow it.

Windows app and website: they connect to the internet only to download calendars you subscribe to by link, directly from the server in that link (for example Google or iCloud): when you subscribe, when you choose Update now, and on the schedule you pick. Like any download, the request shows that server your IP address and that the request comes from Thirteen or your browser; it sends no cookies and does not tell the server which page asked, and it contains nothing about you or your other events. There is no Thirteen server: the download goes straight from your computer or browser to the calendar's host, so we never see the link or the calendar. The links are saved only on your device: on Windows with your calendars, on the website in storage that belongs to Thirteen's website alone. Apart from those downloads, the website only loads its own files from the server that hosts it, and it loads nothing from other sites.

Notifications

On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android, the alerts of events in your device's calendars are shown by the system or your calendar app, as they are for any other calendar app. On Windows, event alerts are scheduled as Windows notifications on your computer. On the website, alerts use your browser's notifications only if you turn them on in Settings, or allow notifications when you first set an alert; the website then installs a small background script (a service worker) whose only job is to show those alerts and open the right day when you click one. It downloads nothing and sends nothing. On Android, if Thirteen shows the alerts for its own "Thirteen" calendar, it asks for permission to send notifications first. Alerts are planned on your device and never sent anywhere. They are local notifications, and no push service is involved.

Settings on your device

Thirteen remembers a few preferences, such as which 13-month calendar you chose, your theme and light or dark mode, whether Gregorian dates are shown in the year grid, which calendars you hide in Thirteen and your default calendar for new events. They are saved only on your device (in the browser's local storage on the web, in the app's own settings elsewhere) and are never sent anywhere by Thirteen. Your device's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include them, as it does for other apps' settings. On iPhone, iPad and Android, removing the app deletes them; on the web, clearing the site's data in your browser does. On a Mac they stay in the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.haugas.thirteen, and on Windows in %APPDATA%\Thirteen, until you delete that folder.

Deleting your data

Events in your Apple or Android calendars belong to those calendars: delete them in Thirteen or in your calendar app. On Windows, your calendars are in the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\Thirteen\Calendars; uninstalling Thirteen keeps them, deleting the folder removes them. On the website, use Settings > Delete all events, or clear the site's data in your browser.

Hosting and app stores

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may record technical details such as IP addresses in its server logs for security. We have no access to those logs; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.

If you install Thirteen from an app store, the store handles the download under its own privacy policy. If you have chosen to share analytics or crash reports with app developers in your device's settings, the platform may pass anonymous crash reports to us. You control that setting; Thirteen adds nothing to it.

Children

Thirteen collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. Earlier versions stay in the project's history on GitHub.
