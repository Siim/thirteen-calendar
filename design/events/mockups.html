Thirteen 1.0 · design review board

Events, in 13 months

Key screens for the events feature on every platform, drawn in the Almanac theme (light, plus one dark frame). Today in every frame is Monday, October 19, 2026, which is IFC Thursday, October 12, 2026, at 09:41 in Tallinn. Every event comes from the shared mockup data set (docs/EVENTS.md §6.7), so screenshots on each platform should match these frames. Board v2 answered the first part of the design review: phone toolbars and month headers are redrawn (A, E), and groups G to L add the states that were missing. Board v3 follows spec v3: details keep Duplicate in the overflow menu, the Calendars lists hide per group, Android edits with Close and an overflow Delete, imports can restore whole calendars, and feeds that return nothing keep their last copy. Board v3.1 follows spec v3.1: the Mac and iPad popover editor (C4), what a repeat change deletes said before the choice (G4), invitee questions and failed held changes (G5), and the Android storage note's conditions (H2). Board v3.2 follows spec v3.2: failed changes say which event and where it was going, and toasts that stay get a Close button (G5); Undo is promised only where it works (G2); the Mac write-only card (E2); the Calendars pane on Android tablets (H1); the date picker at the largest text (J4); and the stale-subscriptions status (L1, L2).

A

Calendar, Month mode

The 4 x 7 month is the hero. Every cell carries both dates.

1Exactly these controls (§2.1). Back goes to Year. The title is a menu with the system choice and Calendars. On the right: List and +. "Today" appears only when today is off screen, so it is hidden here (frame H1 shows it). VoiceOver three-finger swipe and the Previous / Next month actions page without a gesture.
2Both dates, both weekdays. Header line 2 is each column's Gregorian weekday this month: every IFC Sunday in October is a Thursday. Where Gregorian November begins, NOV sits above the 1, never beside a number.
3Dots on narrow phones. Cells under 60 pt show up to three calendar dots, and a count when a day has more than three events (today: 5). Titles live in the day list below; Plus and Pro Max widths show titles in the cells (§2.4).
4The 4-row month leaves room. 28-day months never need a 5th or 6th row, so the selected day's list fits under the grid on every iPhone. Its header opens Day mode. To move an event, long-press its row and drop it on a cell.
5Repeat words show the system. "Every IFC month on the 12th" is a 13-month repeat; "Every 2 weeks on Monday" follows real weeks. The event in progress wears the Now plate.
6Five modes. Day, Week, Month, Year and List in a segmented control on wide windows. ⌃⌘1 to ⌃⌘5 on Mac, Ctrl+Shift+1 to 5 on Windows, D W M Y L on the web.
7Continuation is drawn, not implied. The school break runs past Saturday, so its bar ends in a notch and resumes notched on the next row.
8Details popover. Both dates on a plate, the repeat in 13-month words, Edit and Delete; Duplicate sits in the ••• menu and opens an unsaved draft. Read-only events show a lock and the reason instead; invitations offer Open in Calendar and Edit Alerts (frame G1).
9Calendars in the sidebar. Grouped by account; hiding affects Thirteen only. Subscribed calendars and Birthdays carry a Read only tag.
10Lanes from the cell height. Desktop fits 5 lanes here, so nothing is hidden; a shorter window shows "+3 more" (frame K1). Color is a tint and a 2 px edge, never the text. Header line 2 gives each column's Gregorian weekday.
Interactions. Double-click empty space for a new event at 09:00 (next whole hour today): an unsaved draft, edited in a popover anchored to the cell on Mac and iPad, in the dialog of frame I1 on Windows and web. Drag to move (held until its Undo toast closes). Option or Alt-drag opens a Duplicate draft at the drop, so nothing is saved until Add. Right-click for the context menu. On an iPad by touch, a tap on a chip opens its details and touch and hold makes a draft or starts a drag.

C

Event editor, 13-month dates and repeats

Apple Calendar's editor, with a date field that speaks both calendars.

15Same rows as Apple Calendar. Title, location, all-day, starts, ends, repeat, calendar, two alerts, show as, URL, notes. Title Case only on Apple.
16Dates name their system, captions are Gregorian. The button reads "IFC Thu, Oct 12, 2026" and VoiceOver hears both dates. It opens the picker of C2. Moving Starts keeps the duration and re-derives a 13-month repeat.
17Save is always enabled. With a problem, it moves focus to the field and announces it (frame G3). On an occurrence it asks for the span (frame G2). Swiping the sheet down with changes asks "Discard Changes?" too.
18Slots in picker order. thirteenSlots: Sol between June and July, intercalary days in place (LD only in leap years, YD last). Each cell shows the Gregorian day under the 13-month day.
19Intercalary days are real choices. Picking Leap Day selects the day itself; the grid is replaced by its badge. Stepping to a common year falls back to June 28 and says so in a caption that is announced.
Accessible names. The year and the slot strip are adjustable controls ("October, month 11 of 13", "Leap Day, leap years only"); a cell reads "Thursday, October 12. Gregorian Monday, October 19". The picker opens on the tab last used.
Keyboard. Left and Right by day, through Leap Day and Year Day; Up and Down by row; Page Up and Page Down by month, with Shift by year; typing 2028-LD or 2026-10-19 works on desktop.
20Two calendars, named the same way. "Gregorian calendar" rows are ordinary RRULEs. The 13-month section carries the selected system's name.
21"Next:" is always Gregorian. It shows the real days a 13-month repeat lands on. The footer's second sentence depends on the platform, the calendar and gate V1, and is left out until the gate passes (§2.13). Apple saves FREQ=YEARLY;BYYEARDAY=12,40,...,-18; Android writes RDATE dates.

57Anchored to what you made. A draft dragged out in Week or Day, a double-click in Month, or Edit and Duplicate in a details popover open the editor as a popover on that draft, cell or event (Duplicate: the event it copies). "+" and ⌘N use the sheet. iPad in Slide Over or a narrow split uses the iPhone sheet of C1.
58No popover on a popover. Inside the popover the 13-month date field and the time picker open inline under their row; the repeat, alert and calendar pickers push inside it. At accessibility text sizes the whole editor becomes the sheet.
59Nothing closes it by accident. Once the draft changed, a click outside, Escape, paging, Today or a mode switch asks "Discard This Event?" first (Mac: the popover stops being transient; iPad: interactive dismiss is off while the draft changed). The timeline under it does not scroll while it is open, so the anchor stays in view.
D

Calendars and subscriptions

Device calendars on Apple and Android; local calendars and feeds on Windows and the web.

22Read only is visible before you tap. Subscribed calendars, Birthdays and read-only shares are tagged here and never offered in the editor's calendar picker.
23Thirteen never writes the OS's visibility. Calendar.app and Android keep their own show and hide state (on Android, writing VISIBLE drops the provider's alarms). Hidden calendars are not fetched.
Groups collapse, and hide per group. Each account header carries Hide All (Show All once something in it is hidden); there is no global switch. "Other" is collapsed here and reads "1 calendar, 1 shown"; the state is remembered on this device, so work accounts with forty calendars stay out of the way.
Android adds two rows. "Missing your Google calendars? In the Google Calendar app, open Settings > General and turn on Share Google Calendar data with other apps." and, when nothing is writable, "Create a calendar on this device".
24Stale data stays visible. A feed that fails keeps its last events and says why, with Try again. Feeds refresh on their schedule while the app runs: Windows sends ETag and Last-Modified; the web lets the browser revalidate, so no CORS preflight (offline and stale rows: frame L1).
25The web cannot read Google's links. Google, iCloud and Outlook send no CORS headers, so the web says so plainly, and the dialog's Import .ics file button opens the import flow. Windows subscribes to the same link. No proxy (owner decision, §8.5).
Storage is iCalendar. Each calendar is one .ics document (IndexedDB on the web, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Thirteen\Calendars on Windows), so export is the stored file; an edit rewrites only that event, every imported event stays as it came. The (i) button opens a calendar's settings: name, color, and for feeds the link, the update interval, Update now and Unsubscribe.
E

Calendar access states

Asked in context, never at launch. Everything else keeps working.

26The calendar still works. Without access, the month, Year, Convert, Compare and History stay complete; only events are missing. The card sits where the day's list would be (App Review 5.1.1 iv).
Settings link. iOS opens Thirteen's page in Settings. The Mac opens Privacy & Security > Calendars, falling back to System Settings (gate V7). "+" while access is off shows the same text in an alert with Open Settings.
Same toolbar as A1. Back, the title menu, List and +. People who never want events turn off Settings > Calendar > Show My Events: then no card, no dots and no Up next, and Thirteen never asks.
27One button before Apple's prompt. The pre-alert card has only "Continue"; the first tap on + goes straight to the system prompt. Android may offer "Not now" and asks for read and write together.
Windows and web have no permission. They show storage notes instead: "Your events are saved in this browser on this device. Export them to keep a copy or to move them."

G

Invitations, repeating saves and editor problems

Read-only without dead ends; every save that needs a decision asks for it.

35Read only, with the reason. Invitations keep the lock and the reason line, but no longer end there.
36Two ways out. "Open in Calendar" opens Apple's Calendar on this day (calshow:, gate V21) to reply or change it; Android opens the event itself. "Edit Alerts" changes only this person's alerts where the account keeps them (gate V20). Duplicate sits in the ••• menu.
37Asked for every occurrence edit or move. When the repeat rule itself changed, only "Save for Future Events" and "Cancel" remain, with "Changing the repeat applies to this and all future events." A move of one occurrence is held until its toast closes, so Undo (⌘Z, shake, the toast) discards it. An editor save is written at once, and on Apple and Android a saved change to a repeating event can't be undone, so its "Event saved." toast has no Undo. Future-event changes have no Undo either, so the sheet says what would be lost before the choice (frame G4). A repeat set in another app offers "Save for All Events" instead of future events.
38Save stays enabled. The problem shows under its field with an icon and text (never color alone) and is announced once; Save moves focus to it and saves nothing.
39A conflict is a choice. Keep the date with a yearly repeat Exchange can store, drop the repeat, or pick another calendar.
40Fewer alerts, at once. The Second Alert row disappears as soon as the calendar changes, with the reason, not later on save.
60Say what is lost, before. Book club repeats every IFC month on the 12th; Year Day belongs to no month, so moving its future events there ends the repeat. The sheet says the later events will be deleted and the destructive button repeats it. A series with an end gives the count ("The 7 later events in this series will be deleted."); the first event of a series says "The series becomes one event on Year Day 2026." The editor shows the same line under its Repeat notice.
Losses are counted first. When "future events" would drop changes made to single events after this one, the sheet adds "Changes you made to 2 single events after this one will be removed." (Android, Windows and web count them before the choice; Apple shows its line after gate V23). Future-event changes have no Undo, so nothing is reported after the fact.
61Invitees hear before anything is sent. On Apple and Android, moving or deleting an event you organize says who gets a message, like the editor footer; the pending toast adds "Invitees will be told." Windows and web send nothing, so they don't ask. Illustrative: Sprint planning organized by you with 8 invitees (the data set's only invitation is organized by Marta Kask).
62A held change that fails comes back. Moves and deletes wait for their Undo window (and are written at once a minute before the event's alert). If the write then fails, after one retry, the event returns to its place. When trying again may work, the toast offers "Try again" and no reason; when it can't, it gives the reason and no button. A change written early keeps Undo only where Undo is exact, so an iPhone delete loses it.
63Failures nobody saw name the event. A change that failed while Thirteen was in the background shows at the next launch with its title and where it was going, because the event has quietly gone back. "Try again" writes it at once; for other causes "Show event" opens it. Two or more become one toast whose Show opens the list.
Toasts that stay can be closed. With VoiceOver, Switch Control, AssistiveTouch or TalkBack a toast with an action stays until the next action: another change, paging, Today, a mode or section switch, or opening an event. Every such toast ends with a Close button (44 pt, 48 dp), which writes a held change and never undoes it. With only a hardware keyboard on iPad, toasts stay 20 seconds.
H

Android at 360 dp

Material chrome around the same 13-month grid; the narrowest layout on the board.

41Top app bar. Back to the year, then Today (shown because today is off screen), List, and the overflow menu with the system choice and Calendars. The FAB is New event. TalkBack offers Previous month and Next month on the grid and the title.
Tablets and unfolded foldables. At expanded width the top app bar adds the Day, Week, Month, Year, List buttons, and its overflow menu keeps the system choice and Calendars, which opens the Calendars list as a pane beside the grid (Close or Back hides it). "Show calendars" opens the same pane.
42Dots at 360 dp. DEC sits above the Gregorian 1 inside its cell. Long-press a row of the day list, then drop it on a cell, to move an event.
43Material chrome, same fields. Close (X) on the left, Save on the right, Delete event in the overflow menu, as Google Calendar does. The date names its system; the caption gives the Gregorian day. No URL row: Android's provider has no URL field (§3.3). Back and predictive back ask "Discard changes?" when the draft changed.
44Stored as dates on Android. The provider ignores BYYEARDAY, so 13-month repeats chosen here are written as an RDATE list (in parts of 400 dates when longer), and the Repeat row repeats the picker's line (this series was saved on Android). The line shows only while End repeat is Never; with invitees it says "Because this event has invitees, Thirteen won't add more dates."; on Android 11 and older it stops at 2037. A repeat made on an iPhone keeps its BYYEARDAY rule whatever is edited here (title, time, dates, end, a split, a drag); only a new 13-month choice in the repeat picker turns it into dates, and the picker says so first.
I

Windows and web: editor and import

The same fields in a 560 px dialog; files come in with a plain count.

45Same rows and order as Apple. The date button opens the 13-month picker of C2 as a flyout; typing 2026-M11-12 or 2026-10-19 into it works too.
46Leaving asks first. Escape, Cancel, the close button and Alt+F4 ask "Discard changes?" when the draft changed. After Save, Ctrl+Z undoes it.
47Counts, not jargon. An event and its changed occurrences move together. One already here is updated only when the file's copy was modified later than at its last import (edits in Thirteen don't block it), otherwise counted as unchanged; unreadable ones are skipped and counted on their own line. Every count string has a singular form ("1 event couldn't be read and was skipped.").
Restore. Files exported by Thirteen carry their calendar's id, so importing them restores each calendar with its name and color, and subscriptions with their link. Several calendars export as one file each (a zip on the web, a folder on Windows).
J

Phones: Day, List in 12-hour time, and the largest text

The other compact modes, and what Month and the date picker become at accessibility sizes.

48The week strip names both days. The 13-month week with each Gregorian day under it; on an intercalary day the strip adds that day after Saturday. Swipe, or Previous day / Next day actions, change the day.
49Now on the axis. The line runs under "Sprint planning" and shows between events; Today is hidden because this is today. Touch and hold empty time for a new event at that half hour; on Android a tap draws a "New event" placeholder and a second tap opens it.
50Times follow the device. The time column is sized for "12:30 PM"; each end of a range is formatted on its own ("until 10:30 AM"), never with an interval formatter that adds a dash. Month chips use the short form 9:30a. The U.S. App Store screenshots use 12-hour time.
51Titles, not counts. At accessibility sizes Month becomes a list of days with up to three event lines each and "+2 more", which opens Day. Runs of empty days take one line, "No events, October 21 to October 23". There is no second list under it, Week falls back to Day, and popovers open as sheets.
64The picker becomes a list too. At accessibility sizes the 4 x 7 grid would clip, so the picker opens at the large detent (full screen on Android and the web) and lists the month's 28 days like Month in J3: the 13-month day, the Gregorian day under it, a check on the selected one. The weekday header goes away, because every row names its weekday.
65Stepper and strip stay. The year stepper and the slot strip keep their adjustable controls; the strip scrolls sideways to the selected slot. The footer with the other calendar's date and Done stays pinned. VoiceOver reads each row as in C2: "Thursday, October 12. Gregorian Monday, October 19".
K

Dense days and overlaps

What happens when a day or an hour holds more than fits.

52"+3 more" opens the whole day. All-day first, then by time, in the rows of the phone's day list. Rows open details, drag onto the grid, and take Delete. VoiceOver reads the button as "3 more events on Thursday, October 12, 2026"; closing the popover returns focus to it.
Keyboard. Arrows move between days; Enter or F2 moves into a day's events, arrows or Tab move among them, Escape goes back to the day.
53Blocks keep a minimum width. 48 pt in Week, 64 pt in Day. A cluster that does not fit shows its first slots and a "+8" block over the hidden events' time; it lists them in a popover ("8 more events at 14:00").
L

Windows and web: storage, offline and recovery

Local data has its own failure modes; each one gets one plain sentence.

54One offline banner. Refreshes pause and rows show no network errors until the connection returns.
55Stale is a warning. A daily feed not updated for two days turns its time into a warning, whatever the reason. While any visible feed is stale, the Calendar toolbar in every mode also shows "Subscriptions not updated" (L2), so outdated events in Month, List or Up next never pass unmarked, even when this list is behind the title menu on a phone.
56Safari's one-week rule, said plainly. In a Safari tab the card names the cause and the fix (Dock or Home Screen, support page). Other browsers get "Keep my data" (L2).
No local calendar left? The next "+" or import creates "Personal" again, and the default calendar moves to the first local calendar.
Errors keep the data. A broken .ics is renamed .broken-…, never overwritten; the web offers the raw text with "Download it".
